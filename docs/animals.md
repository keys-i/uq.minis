# Animal anatomy and movement

The 50 terminal animals use species descriptions, museum specimens, conservation photographs and locomotion research as modelling constraints
The sculptures remain a shaded side-view interpretation on a braille grid, rather than motion-capture models or exact scientific reconstructions

## Species constraints

The table records anatomy and behaviour to preserve, rather than claiming that every referenced detail or measured joint trajectory fits the terminal drawing
The huia represents a female, the Nilgiri tahr an adult saddle-back male and the rusty patched bumblebee a worker
A single sculpture does not represent every sex, age or seasonal form

## Joint mechanics and evidence

Forelimbs and hind limbs bend in different directions, with the torso moving above planted feet
Walking separates stance from an eased lift and swing, while trotting pairs opposite fore and hind feet
The [dog muscle study](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/215/2/287/11087/Activity-of-extrinsic-limb-muscles-in-dogs-at-walk) and [quadrupedal phasing study](https://elifesciences.org/articles/29495) support those distinctions; their measurements are not presented as African wild dog or cat measurements
Heavy animals retain smaller body pitch, and the elephant's same-side hind foot precedes its forefoot
The [elephant limb study](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/211/17/2735/17733/The-movements-of-limb-segments-and-joints-during) and [locomotion study](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/213/5/694/10070/Biomechanics-of-locomotion-in-Asian-elephants) guide the flexing joints and weight transfer
Hopping separates crouch, airborne extension and recovery; the [frog jumping study](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/209/1/66/33403/Take-off-and-landing-forces-in-jumping-frogs) concerns other frogs, rather than measured mountain chicken kinematics

Feathered wings have a shoulder, elbow and wrist, with primary feathers attached at the distal wing and secondaries along the forearm
The upstroke changes extension and fan spread as well as shoulder angle
Pigeons keep a narrower pointed fan than vultures and eagles, and soaring uses small adjustments instead of the full flutter stroke
[Pigeon flight experiments](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/213/10/1651/9685/Wing-and-body-kinematics-of-takeoff-and-landing), [kestrel joint tracking](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/227/15/jeb247305/361474/Steady-as-they-hover-kinematics-of-kestrel-wing) and [slotted-tip experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5454299/) guide those mechanics
Kestrel and rock-pigeon measurements do not establish exact huia, passenger-pigeon or Philippine-eagle kinematics
Wingbeat rates in the artwork are display timing choices, with gentle body heave, head stabilisation and tail adjustment accompanying the wing motion

Aquatic mammals move the body and flukes together, while a fixed dorsal fin follows the torso
Fish and reptile lateral tail bends use a foreshortened side-view projection rather than a whale-like upward wag
Penguin flippers remain distinct from legs, with distal bending informed by [penguin swimming research](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.242140) rather than feather-fan mechanics
Those measurements concern other penguin species, rather than a fitted African penguin trajectory
The [aquatic control-surface review](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/220/23/4351/33683/Control-surfaces-of-aquatic-vertebrates-active-and) explains why fins, flippers and flukes need different shapes and motions

## Reconstruction limits

The [WWF and Tamil Nadu tahr survey](https://wwfin.awsassets.panda.org/downloads/status_and_distribution_of_the_nilgiri_tahr_in_the_western_ghats__india_final.pdf) distinguishes age and sex through horn size, coat and saddle markings
[Duke's aye-aye account](https://lemur.duke.edu/discover/meet-the-lemurs/aye-aye/) constrains the probing finger and listening behaviour, while [NOAA's vaquita](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/vaquita) and [blue whale](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/species/blue-whale) accounts distinguish their dorsal fins, heads and body proportions
The [River Dolphins account](https://www.riverdolphins.org/river-dolphins-worldwide/ganges-river-dolphin/) describes the Ganges dolphin's low dorsal ridge, flexible neck, long rostrum and pinhole-sized eye

Extinct animals lack contemporary recordings for fitting a complete movement model, and historical footage is limited even where it survives
Skeletons, skins, soft tissue and historical descriptions constrain appearance, but do not determine every joint angle, contour or behaviour
The dodo is informed by [skeletal research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5721909/) and the [Oxford specimen](https://oumnh.ox.ac.uk/learn-the-oxford-dodo), which preserves head and foot soft tissue
Rare living species also lack complete gait measurements in the references used here
No claim of exact scientific reconstruction, complete anatomy or measured species-specific timing should be inferred from the artwork

Reference pages come from museums, conservation organisations, government agencies and research publishers
Some sources cover several species or describe historical observations, rather than current conservation status
The linked name opens that reference while preserving the current input
Blurb bubbles are fictional first-person remarks, separate from factual descriptions and conservation status

## Editing and verification

Edit [anatomy.toml](assets/terminal/anatomy.toml) for local contours, limb types and wing proportions, [sculpt.py](assets/terminal/sculpt.py) for joints, and [forest.toml](assets/terminal/forest.toml) for metadata, colours and blurbs
Hard outlines preserve pointed horns, curved bills, fin edges, tapering tails and shell notches
Soft outlines add shoulder, flank and belly curvature without making every part an ellipse
Markings are clipped to their moving host, while joint connectors keep limbs, feather roots and tails attached
Natural coats retain subtle flag-coloured highlights and surroundings

Static flags use the mascot's braille dot size in three terminal rows, twelve dots high, beside status, country and habitat
Each country in a shared range has its own flag map; aspect ratios are rounded to the nearest terminal dot
Every dot inside a flag's outline stays filled, including cells containing more than one source colour
The small grid simplifies fine emblems, and a terminal cell can carry only one foreground colour
Flag dots retain the original vector colours, while dark country lettering receives a small contrast lift
Flag artwork comes from [FlagCDN](https://flagcdn.com), checked against the [Japan Ministry of Foreign Affairs flag chart](https://www.mofa.go.jp/mofaj/kids/kokki/files/national_flags_list.pdf)
Regional silhouettes use [Natural Earth boundaries](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/110m-cultural-vectors/110m-admin-0-countries/), whose [map data is public domain](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)
Ocean labels use orthographic globe silhouettes; these and regional outlines are geographic icons rather than official flags
The maps and palettes in [flags.toml](assets/terminal/flags.toml) load offline without an image library or network request

Rebuild with `uv run --directory backend python assets/terminal/sculpt.py`; appending species names rebuilds only their movement files while refreshing the shared still catalogue
The generator samples matching light, material and pointer maps at **224 × 160 dots**, then the terminal resamples them to its actual cells
Movement runs at **24 frames per second**, with bounded runtime caches and no live geometry generation
The offline bake uses up to four processes, with the parent publishing complete compressed assets atomically

Regression checks cover contour validation, concave edges, fixed horns and dorsal fins, clipped markings, connected limb and feather joints, diagonal trot timing, coordinated movement, flightless species and eyelid differences
Visual review includes resting poses and non-equally-spaced movement phases, avoiding accidental sampling of the same wingbeat phase

## Runtime measurements

On this Mac with Python 3.15, 64 uncached numbat frames measured at 40 × 16 cells had a 2.03 ms median and 3.14 ms maximum, compared with 2.09 ms and 3.21 ms before the larger source artwork
At 73 × 26 cells the median was 5.80 ms and maximum 6.64 ms, compared with 5.88 ms and 6.78 ms before
Both stay below the 41.7 ms frame budget at 24 FPS; these small differences do not establish a speedup
Parsing the first movement file with an empty in-process cache took 97.9 ms, compared with 37.2 ms before; the first menu paint uses the still sculpture before parsing movement
Tracemalloc measured 25.36 MiB for 144 cached portraits, compared with 26.22 MiB before
Six parsed movement files retained 82.84 MiB with a 103.93 MiB peak in a separate run; the movement cache remains bounded at six files
